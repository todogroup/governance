# OSPO for Agents WG - Frequently Asked Questions

## About the Working Group

### What is the OSPO for Agents Working Group?

The OSPO for Agents Working Group develops comprehensive guidance, case studies, patterns, and reference materials to help organizations implement effective Open Source Program Office (OSPO) practices for managing AI agents and agent-based systems.

### Why do we need this working group now?

AI agents are increasingly being used in software development and enterprise operations, but OSPO practices for governing them are still evolving. Organizations lack:
- Clear governance frameworks for agents
- Case studies showing how peers manage this
- Best practices and decision models
- Compliance and risk guidance specific to agents

### Who should join this working group?

Anyone interested in OSPO practices for AI agents, including:
- OSPO professionals and leaders
- Enterprise architects
- Security and compliance specialists
- Open source practitioners
- AI/ML engineers
- Researchers
- Consultants working with organizations on OSPOs

**You do NOT need to**:
- Be an expert in AI/agents
- Work for a specific company size
- Have existing OSPO experience
- Be a TODO Group member (though you're welcome to join!)

### How is this different from other AI governance initiatives?

This WG focuses specifically on **OSPO governance of agents**, not:
- General AI/ML ethics (being addressed by other initiatives)
- Technical AI safety/alignment
- Licensing of foundation models (though we touch on this)
- AI policy/regulation

Our angle is: **How do existing OSPO practices need to evolve to handle agent use cases?**

**Minimum**:

Absolutely! We need:
- **Architects**: Help design patterns

See [CONTRIBUTING.md](./CONTRIBUTING.md) for detailed guidelines. Quick ways to contribute:

1. **Share your experience**: Write a case study about your organization's agent governance
2. **Document patterns**: Help document governance patterns you've observed
3. **Provide feedback**: Review draft materials and comment
4. **Organize content**: Help structure and curate resources
5. **Expertise**: Share specific knowledge (compliance, security, architecture, etc.)

### What's the process for proposing ideas?

1. **Small ideas**: Share in #ospo-for-agents Slack
2. **Larger initiatives**: Create a GitHub discussion
3. **Content contributions**: Use templates in /templates directory
4. **Discussion topics**: Suggest in meeting agenda or Slack

All ideas are welcome! If it aligns with the WG scope, it will be considered.

## Working Group Structure

### Who leads the working group?

- **Chair**: SC member (assigned by Steering Committee)
- **Co-chairs**: Volunteers from community (1-2 approved by Chair)
- **Work Stream Leads**: Volunteers leading specific work areas
- **Contributing Members**: Everyone else!

### How are decisions made?

1. **Small decisions**: Chat in Slack (#ospo-for-agents)
2. **Medium decisions**: Discussed in bi-weekly meetings, consensus-based
3. **Large decisions**: Formal discussion in GitHub, WG lead makes call with SC input
4. **Charter changes**: SC approval required

We aim for consensus but won't let one person block progress.

### What are the meeting schedules?

- **Bi-weekly sync**: [Time TBD - will be posted in Slack]
- **Asynchronous work**: GitHub, Slack, discussions
- **Monthly SC check-in**: WG lead reports to Steering Committee
- **Ad-hoc work streams**: As needed for specific projects

All meetings recorded for asynchronous participation.

### How do I provide feedback on direction/decisions?

1. **In meetings**: Speak up!
2. **In Slack**: Post thoughts in #ospo-for-agents
3. **In writing**: Create a GitHub discussion
4. **Anonymously**: DM the WG chair or co-chair
5. **Officially**: Request WG chair include in SC feedback

## About the Work & Deliverables

### What are Phase 1 deliverables?

Within 6 months, we aim to publish:

1. **Governance Framework**: Decision models and frameworks for agent governance
2. **Case Studies**: 5 real-world examples from organizations
3. **Governance Patterns**: 7 reusable patterns for common scenarios
4. **Best Practices Guide**: Comprehensive guidance for OSPO managers
5. **Reference Materials**: Templates, checklists, compliance tools

### How are case studies selected?

We're seeking case studies from organizations that can share:
- Their challenges with agent governance
- Solutions they implemented
- Results and lessons learned
- Can be written/interviews or co-created with WG

**No specific company size/type required** - we want diverse perspectives!

**Anonymity available** if needed.

### What if my organization wants to contribute a case study?

Great! See [Case Study Template](./templates/case-study-template.md) for what we need.

Next steps:
1. Open an issue in GitHub with rough outline
2. Discuss in #ospo-for-agents
3. Add to case study tracking
4. Schedule interview/kickoff
5. Write first draft
6. Review and iterate

We can help with writing if needed!

### I have ideas for topics - how do I suggest them?

Post in #ospo-for-agents or create a GitHub discussion. We track proposed topics and incorporate where relevant.

## Quality & Standards

### How is content quality ensured?

1. **Peer Review**: Multiple reviewers before publication
2. **SME Validation**: Subject matter experts review technical accuracy
3. **Community Feedback**: Draft sharing for comments
4. **SC Oversight**: Steering Committee reviews major outputs
5. **Iterative Process**: Updates as we learn and evolve

### Can we use real company names in case studies?

Yes, but we protect confidentiality:
- Published only with explicit permission
- Can anonymize if preferred
- Can discuss in closed setting first
- Authors decide what level of detail to share

### Who owns the content that's created?

All content is contributed under TODO Group licenses:
- **Code**: Apache License 2.0
- **Documentation/Content**: Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 (CC-BY-4.0)

You retain attribution, but content is openly available, modifiable, and shareable.

### Can our company use this internally before public release?

Yes! We encourage organizations to:
- Use draft materials internally
- Test patterns and frameworks
- Provide feedback
- Contribute case studies based on experience

Just note that materials are work-in-progress until officially published.

## TODO Group Context

### What's TODO Group's relationship to Linux Foundation?

TODO Group is a Linux Foundation project. The LF provides:
- Legal/governance support
- Community management
- Infrastructure (Slack, GitHub repos, websites)
- Antitrust guidance
- Financial support for initiatives

This WG operates under TODO Group governance, which itself operates under LF policies.

The OSPO for Agents WG **complements** other initiatives:

- **OSPO Metrics WG (CHAOSS)**: Our patterns will reference relevant metrics

Yes, and we'll coordinate with:
- **OpenChain**: License compliance work (often relevant to agents)

## Governance & Support

### Who can be WG chair?

Must be (or become) a TODO Group General Member and approved by Steering Committee. Typically:
- An experienced OSPO practitioner
- Has capacity for leadership role
- Willing to serve ~2 year term
- Strong communication skills

### What support does the WG get?

- TODO Group **Project Manager** for operational support
- **GitHub organization** for repositories
- **Communication channels** (Slack, mailing list)
- **Marketing support** for announcements
- **Community platform** for engagement
- **SC sponsor** for guidance and decisions

### Can the WG request funding?

Yes - the TODO Group and sponsors can provide funding for:
- Research studies
- External expertise
- Event sponsorships
- Tools/platforms

Submit requests to the WG Chair or SC for discussion.

### What happens if the WG becomes inactive?

Per the charter, if the WG has no activity for 3+ months, the SC may:
1. Attempt to restart/revive the WG
2. Transfer leadership
3. Retire the WG (archive materials publicly)

Your work is never lost - contributions remain publicly available.
